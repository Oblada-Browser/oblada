# Oblada 0.3.0: devlog

Released on 2026-10-22. Based on Chromium 156.0.8078.48. What changed since 0.2.0, in detail; the
public release notes are in [`changelogs/0_3_0.json`](../changelogs/0_3_0.json).

## Workspace themes

- Each workspace has a theme (`oblada::workspaces::Theme`, `workspaces/workspace_theme.{h,cc}`),
  which the window takes while it shows the workspace, as with Zen's and Arc's color
  pickers: up to three colors (`dots`), a gradient over the window's glass from the top
  left to the bottom right; how much they cover the glass (`opacity`, unset for the
  window's default: Chromium's 0.22 light, 0.30 dark); `grain`; and the scheme, light, dark
  or automatic (as the browser's mode, the system's by default).
- A color is where the picker places it: its hue an angle, its saturation a distance from
  the middle. How light it is depends on the scheme (`ColorOf()`): deep in a dark window
  (lightness 0.34), pastel in a light one (0.80), so the window's text reads on it either
  way, and the colors follow the system when it turns dark. Dots after the first are placed
  by a harmony (`Harmonize()`: analogous, complementary or triadic); dragging one moves the
  others along (`MoveDot()`).
- **A default theme and the workspaces' own.** The profile has a default theme
  (`oblada.workspaces.default_theme`; `DefaultTheme()`, `SetDefaultTheme()`,
  `ResetDefaultTheme()`), which starts without colors (a gray window), at the window's
  opacity, with a little grain (the dial's second step, 2/15; `FirstDefaultTheme()`). A workspace has it
  unless it has a theme of its own (`Workspace::theme`, optional; `ThemeOf()`). Saved with
  the workspace (`oblada.workspaces.list`): its own as a `theme` dictionary (`dots` as
  `[hue, distance]` pairs, `harmony`, `scheme`, `opacity`, `grain`), else
  `"default_theme": true`. It replaces "Color the window" (`tints_window`): a workspace saved
  before themes that colored the window gets its color as its own theme
  (`ThemeFromGroupColor()`), one that didn't has the default theme. New workspaces have the
  default theme.
- **Where they're edited.** A right click on the sidebar (the window's system menu, patched
  in `system_menu_model_builder.cc` and `system_menu_model_delegate.cc`, a new patch): "Edit
  default theme…", the picker by the sidebar's top, over the page
  (`ShowDefaultThemePicker()`), and "Reset default theme" (enabled once it's changed). A
  workspace's menu: "Edit theme…" (it gets its own theme as it's changed, starting from the
  default one) and "Use the default theme", checked while it has it (unchecking gives it its
  own, a copy of the default one). Incognito windows don't take themes, nor edit the
  default one.
- **Applying it.** `WorkspaceSwitcher::ApplyTheme()` (in `Rebuild()`, and on theme
  changes, e.g. the system turning dark) sets the window's color mode for a light or dark
  scheme (`Widget::SetColorModeOverride()`), its other colors from the first color (the user
  color override, as before; gray without one) and its background
  (`oblada::window_background::Set()`). Incognito windows and windows with an extension's
  theme keep their own look.
- **The background** (`ui/window_background.h`, `window_background_mac.mm`). On macOS the
  window's glass has a tint view over it (`BrowserNativeWidgetMac`); the patch to
  `browser_native_widget_mac.mm` has Oblada paint it (`Paint()`), with the frame color and
  Chromium's opacity for windows without a theme. Two Core Animation layers in it: a
  `CAGradientLayer` with the colors at the theme's opacity, and the grain, a tile of light
  and dark specks (made once, 128 points, Retina) as a pattern color, at up to 0.5 opacity.
  Changes fade in 0.3 s, e.g. switching workspace; resizing doesn't animate. The state is
  kept with the `NSWindow` (an associated object). A forced scheme also sets the window's
  `appearance`, for the glass to be dark or light with it. Other systems have no glass yet
  (`window_background_other.cc`).
- **The glass shows what's behind the window.** Under the glass, Chromium 156 puts an
  opaque view (`OpaqueFrameBackgroundView`, in the frame color) over the part of the window
  the glass doesn't cover, worked out from the glass's width or height
  (`UpdateBackgroundGeometry()`). Oblada's glass covers the whole window, neither limited,
  and the opaque view was left as big as the window: the glass blurred that solid gray
  rather than the desktop, whatever the opacity. The patch to
  `browser_native_widget_mac.mm` gives it no size then. Checked in a visible window (the
  snapshots can't show the glass): the sidebar shows the desktop, blurred.
- **The picker** (`workspaces/theme_picker.{h,cc}`, "Edit theme…" in a workspace's menu, in
  place of "Color the window"): a bubble by the workspace's button or header. At the top of
  a field with a dotted grid, the scheme (sparkles, sun, moon); in it, the colors, placed by a
  click (the first, or moving it there) and dragged; at its bottom, add a color, remove one,
  and the next harmony. Below, the presets (`Presets()`): four pages of eight, soft, vivid,
  two and three colors, with arrows that go round. Then the opacity, a slider drawn as a
  wave (wavy as far as the value, flat past it, its knob riding the wave; a double click goes
  back to the default) and the grain, a dial of 16 steps clockwise from the top (it stops at
  the ends rather than going round). The glyphs are drawn, not icons; the sliders take arrow
  keys. Every change is saved at once (`UpdateWorkspace()`), so the window follows as the
  user drags.
- **Snapshots** draw the window's background under its views (`window_background::Render()`,
  the tint view's layers on a gray standing for the glass), instead of leaving the sidebar
  see-through.
- New debug actions: `ws-theme` (the picker), `ws-theme-dots:`, `ws-theme-scheme:`,
  `ws-theme-opacity:`, `ws-theme-grain:` (giving the workspace its own theme),
  `ws-theme-default`, `default-theme` (its picker), `default-theme-reset`; `ws-tint:` is
  gone. The smoke test checks the picker's buttons and presets, the window taking a theme's
  opacity, grain and scheme, and the default theme.

## Sidebar and address bar

- **Three window layouts** (`oblada/browser/ui/sidebar_mode.{h,cc}`), the same in every
  window of the profile (`oblada.sidebar.mode`), picked in Settings > Oblada or the app
  menu's "Window layout ▸" (`ModeMenuModel`, in place of the "Compact mode" item):
  - **Sidebar**, the default: the full sidebar, as before.
  - **Scaffold**: the toolbar is a row at the top of the window, next to the window
    controls, as Chromium's (`oblada::sidebar::SetScaffoldToolbar()`), without the profile
    and extensions buttons (`toolbar_view.cc`), which are in the app menu and the site
    panel; the tabs' icons are a column below it, left of the page, with the page's
    margins around them, without expanding on hover. The page is right below the toolbar,
    without its top margin: as far from the address bar as the window's top is.
  - **Compact**: only the page, with its margins; the sidebar shows over it when the
    pointer reaches the window's left edge (below).
  Scaffold and compact are Chromium's collapsed vertical tab strip, sidebar its expanded
  one. A window starts collapsed or not as the layout says, whatever its session saved
  (`vertical_tab_strip_state_controller_impl.cc`, `CollapsesTabStrip()`): asking the strip
  to collapse before it's laid out leaves its controller expanded. Afterwards each window's
  tab strip region keeps the two in step (`ModeKeeper`, made by
  `vertical_tab_strip_region_view.cc`): a new layout collapses or expands the strip, and
  collapsing it (⌘⇧L, the sidebar's menu) picks the collapsed layout last chosen
  (`oblada.sidebar.collapsed_mode`, compact at first), expanding it the sidebar. The keeper
  marks the region with the layout (`ModeOf()`) for the browser layout
  (`browser_view_tabbed_layout_impl.cc`: `TransientLayoutData::oblada_scaffold`), the
  region's own (no row of buttons above the tabs, `IsObladaSidebar()`) and its hover
  (none in scaffold mode). New debug options: `screenshot.mjs --mode=scaffold|compact`
  (`--compact` is `--mode=compact`), and the action `mode:<layout>`.
- **The welcome asks for a layout** (`oblada/browser/welcome/`), right after the import:
  three little windows, each animated as its layout works (another tab picked in the
  sidebar; another tab's icon picked in scaffold mode, under the address bar; the pointer
  reaching the left edge in compact mode and the sidebar widening over the page), the one
  in use marked. Clicking one sets it at once (the page's `getLayout` and `setLayout`
  messages, `sidebar::ModeName()` and `ModeFromName()`), the window changing around the
  page; a note says Settings > Oblada changes it later. It replaces the step about compact
  mode. `oblada://welcome/#<step>` now opens any step with an id, not just `#import`
  (`#layout` for this one). The smoke test picks scaffold there on the first launch.
- **The compact sidebar is a card that widens over the page**
  (`oblada/browser/ui/sidebar_card.{h,cc}`), from the window's left edge:
  - **Hidden**, the narrow tab strip is out of the window but for the page's margin (8
    points, `oblada::sidebar::CardMargin()`) at its left edge, where the pointer expands
    it on hover.
  - **It opens sooner**: 80 ms after the pointer stops there (`tabs/features.cc`), rather
    than once Chromium's velocity heuristic finds the pointer has slowed down enough
    (about 150 to 250 ms from entering it, before the animation), though not as the
    pointer only passes over it.
  - **Expanding**, the card widens with Chromium's expand-on-hover width (250 ms) up to
    the sidebar's width, and what's in it is laid out at that width from the start
    (`TransientLayoutData::oblada_overlay`, while that width isn't 0), clipped to the card
    (`ClipToCard()`, the layers' clip and rounded corners). The toolbar comes down from
    the card's top as the tabs move down under it, fading in (Chromium's expand-on-hover
    opacity, 100 ms). The window controls show once it's down (95% of the width), already
    in the card's corner, moved there by its margin (`oblada::sidebar::SetWindowControls()`,
    which moves the buttons from where AppKit puts them, again whenever it puts them back,
    and before showing them), as far from its corner as from the window's in the full
    sidebar; they go as soon as it starts shrinking. Shrinking, the same backwards. (Coming
    down with the toolbar and fading in, moved and faded at every frame, they flashed.)
  - **Its look**: the window's, its workspace's theme (the gradient in the window's
    coordinates, as the glass shows it, at the theme's opacity, and the grain), on a gray
    standing for the glass (the window's `GlassStandIn()`), opaque, with an edge and no
    shadow; its corners the page's (10 points: rounder ones, concentric with the window
    controls in it, didn't match the page next to it). In a window without glass
    (`in_glass_mode()`), it's the frame color.
  - The card is drawn by Chromium's blur backdrop, the view behind the vertical tab strip
    (`VerticalTabStripBackgroundBlurBackdrop`, which `browser_view.cc` now makes also
    without macOS 26's glass): the layout gives it the card's bounds and `SetCard()`; its
    `OnPaint()` calls `PaintCard()`. The tab strip and the toolbar are see-through over
    it, without their outline, shadow and blur.
  - Before this, the compact sidebar went through two versions in this release: a rail of
    the tabs' icons in a card, from which a panel faded in, then which widened into the
    panel (its colors jumping from the rail's, see-through on the glass, to the panel's,
    the page showing through it, and its buttons jumping from a column to a row). The
    workspace switcher's buttons can still be laid out between their column and their
    row (`WorkspaceSwitcher::SetExpansion()`, from `oblada::sidebar::SetExpansion()` on
    the region view): a column in scaffold mode, a row otherwise.
  - **The address bar floats in compact mode too.** Hidden, the toolbar had no size (since
    compact mode was made): its address bar took the focus (⌘L, or a new tab's ⌘T) but was
    laid out 0×0, so it never showed over the page. It's now laid out at the sidebar's
    width just out of the window, left of it, and floats over the page from there
    (`oblada::sidebar::LayOutLocationBar()` places it from the page's bounds).
  - The window's grain tile is made once for both (`window_background::GrainTile()`, in
    the new `window_background.cc`), the Core Animation pattern and the card's Skia
    shader.
  - Snapshots of the window are cropped to it (`CopyOutputRequest::set_area()`): a shadow
    reaching out of it made them bigger, off by its size, and left out the window's
    background.
  - The smoke test checks the three layouts, ⌘⇧L between sidebar and compact, and the
    address bar floating in compact mode.

- **Full screen, the window controls' bar shows over the sidebar and the page**: as the
  pointer reaches the top of the screen, they move down only out of the way of the menu bar
  (`browser_frame_view_mac.mm`), no longer of the bar with the window controls below it too,
  which covers the sidebar's row of buttons while it's there. Moving down by both, as in
  Safari, the content jumped up by the bar's height when the pointer left the menu bar for
  the bar below it.
- **The workspace's header** (`tab_group_header_view.cc`) reads as a heading of the tabs
  below rather than as one of them: its name a size smaller and semibold, in the tabs' text
  color at 65%, its icon 14 points (in the 16 points of the favicon column), and a line under
  it.
- **The workspace's header is hidden by default**: the workspaces at the bottom of the
  sidebar tell which one is open, and have its menu. Settings > Oblada turns it
  on (`oblada.workspaces.show_header`, `workspaces::ShowsHeader()`). The tab strip's region
  view tells the state controller whether the sidebar shows headers (the user wants them and
  it looks expanded: `SetObladaShowingHeaders()`, which replaces `SetObladaShownExpanded()`,
  only the header used it); the workspace switcher watches the pref and has the region laid
  out again. Hidden, the header leaves no room above the tabs (`tab_group_view_layout.cc`).
  A new debug action, `ws-header`, turns it on.
- **Settings > Oblada** (`oblada://settings/oblada`), a page of Oblada's own settings,
  first in Settings' menu with Oblada's logo; for now its Appearance section: the window's
  layout (radio buttons, `oblada.sidebar.mode`) and the workspace's header. Its element,
  `settings-oblada-page`, is a module Oblada serves itself
  (`oblada/browser/settings/oblada_page.{h,cc}`, `AddObladaPage()`, a request filter on
  Settings' data source for `oblada/oblada_page.js`), as Oblada adds no files to Chromium's
  tree and Settings' lint wants an element per file: a Lit element drawing into the page's
  tree (so Settings' styles apply) with Settings' own controls, which bind to preferences by
  name, and a search of its own (`searchContents()`, matching its strings). Settings imports
  it from `settings_main.ts` as `/oblada/oblada_page.js`, which its `BUILD.gn` maps to a
  declaration for the compiler (`oblada_page.d.ts`, as Chromium does for `/strings.m.js`)
  and leaves out of the bundle (`optimize_webui_excludes`); `route.ts` and `router.ts` add
  `routes.OBLADA`, `settings_main.html` its view and `settings_menu.html` its item;
  `prefs_util.cc` lets it change its preferences. It replaces a first try in this release,
  Settings > Appearance > Oblada, a subpage of the Appearance page.
- **The container chip** (`containers/container_chip.cc`) shows only the container's icon,
  in a circle of its color (a dot for a container without an icon), its name in the tooltip
  and for screen readers: with the name, up to 120 points wide, it left no room for the
  address and could be cut off at the bar's end. A temporary container has its number
  instead ("Temporary 2" shows 2, `containers::TemporaryNumber()`, even once renamed), which
  tells it from the others, whose icon they share. A new debug action, `temporary`, opens a
  tab in a new temporary container.
- **A container tab's mark** (`PaintContainerIndicator()`) is 2 points wide against the
  tab's edge (1.5 points in), 6.5 points from the favicon instead of 4.
- **The site's information** is the site panel's (its security button, now with the tooltip
  "View site information"): in the sidebar, the address bar shows no location icon for a
  site without warnings, hovered or not, but while it's edited, when the icon is the
  suggestion's (`LocationBarView::IsObladaSiteInfoInPanel()`). Sites with warnings keep their
  "Not secure" chip. A new debug action, `site-panel`, opens the panel (and takes the bar out
  of its quiet look, as a click on the button would): `site_panel::ShowPanel()`.
- **Settings' Appearance page** has no "Theme" (`page_visibility.ts`: `setTheme` off) nor
  "Customize your toolbar" (`appearance_page.html.ts`, a new patch): workspaces have themes,
  and the toolbar is the sidebar's row of buttons.
- **No "Send feedback"** in the window's menu (right click on the sidebar,
  `system_menu_model_builder.cc`): it opened Google's feedback form. Its command,
  `IDC_VERTICAL_TABS_SEND_FEEDBACK`, is disabled (`browser_command_controller.cc`).

## Peek

- **Extensions see a peek's page as a tab** (`oblada/browser/peek/peek.cc`). Its page was a
  bare `WebContents`, given a tab's helpers only once made a tab: while it loaded,
  extensions saw its requests as no tab's (`tabId` -1) and its content scripts without a
  `sender.tab`, so an ad blocker (uBlock Origin) couldn't filter it, nor inject its scripts,
  and the page stayed that way once a tab. It now gets a tab's helpers as it's created
  (`PeekTabHelpers`, a friend of `TabHelpers` as Chromium's Link Preview is:
  `tab_helpers.h`, a new patch), as a page prerendered for a tab does, and keeps its tab id
  when it becomes one. The tabs APIs find it as they find a prerendered page, a tab in no
  window at no index (`ExtensionTabUtil::GetTabById()`, through
  `peek::FindPeekByTabId()`), and `tabs.executeScript()`/`insertCSS()` run in it though it
  has no window (`tabs_api.cc`); `scripting.executeScript()` needed only the lookup.
- **Full screen from a peek makes its page a tab, in full screen**. The peek's delegate
  didn't answer full screen requests, which Chromium then takes for granted: the request
  was left pending, and the page never went full screen again, also once a tab (YouTube's
  button did nothing). `PeekController::EnterFullscreenModeForTab()` makes the page a tab
  at once, without the card's animation (`CloseNow(keep_page)`, `MakeTab()`), and passes
  the request on to the window, which now shows it; a refusal there would be told to the
  page (`ExitFullscreen()`).
- A new debug action, `peek-tab`, makes the open peek a tab (`peek::OpenPeekAsTab()`). The
  smoke test checks that an extension sees a peek's requests as a tab's and runs a script
  in it, and that full screen asked for in a peek gives a tab in full screen.

## Clean links

- **"Copy Clean Link"**, as Firefox's: a link copied without the parameters sites add to it
  to track who follows it (`oblada/browser/clean_links/`, `clean_links::Clean()`).
  - **The lists** are Firefox's strip-on-share lists, unchanged
    (`clean_links/lists/`: `MPL2/StripOnShare.json`, Mozilla's, and
    `LGPL/StripOnShareLGPL.json` with its license; `lists/README.md` says from which
    revision and how to update them). `sources.gni` writes them into the generated
    `oblada/clean_links_lists.h` as raw strings, as it does `version.h`, and they're parsed
    once, when first needed. Each has a global rule and rules for some sites, by host or by
    registrable domain; as Firefox, a site in both lists has the parameters of both.
  - **Mozilla's list** (`clean_links/mozilla_list.{h,cc}`), which Firefox strips from pages'
    addresses as they load and adds to the global rule for copying: the `query-stripping`
    collection of Firefox's Remote Settings. As in Firefox it's built in
    (`lists/mozilla/query-stripping.json`, the server's answer of 2026-10-22, also in the
    generated header) and kept up to date from Mozilla's server: a minute after the browser
    starts if it wasn't downloaded in the last day, then daily, an hour later after a
    failure. The download (`SimpleURLLoader` on the browser's own network context, no
    cookies, its traffic annotation `oblada_clean_links_mozilla_list`) is read as Firefox
    reads it, the `stripList` of each record (also as names separated by spaces, its old
    form), up to a thousand names without spaces or query separators, and kept in Local
    State (`oblada.clean_links.mozilla_list`, `…_downloaded`), used instead of the built-in
    copy from the next start. Firefox checks its records' signature; Oblada relies on
    https. `--oblada-mozilla-list-url=<url>` downloads it from elsewhere at once (tests).
  - **Cleaning** follows Firefox's `URLQueryStringStripper::StripForCopyOrShare()`: a web
    link's parameters are told by their name as written, in any case; those in the global
    rule or the link's host's or site's are removed, and the others kept as they were, in
    their order, with the fragment. A parameter whose value is a link (a redirect's
    `?url=…`) has that link cleaned too, one level deep. A link left with no parameters loses
    its `?`. Firefox's other list, of parameters it strips as pages load, which it adds to
    the global rule, is Mozilla's list (below).
  - **Where**: a link's context menu has it after "Copy link address", enabled when the link
    has something to remove (`render_view_context_menu.cc`, counted in Chromium's metrics as
    the link copied), and the address bar's after "Copy", for the page's link while the bar
    shows it (`omnibox_context_menu_mixin.cc`, a new patch), as in Firefox. "Copy link"
    (`IDC_COPY_URL`, the site panel's button) copies the page's link clean, as Zen's "Copy
    current URL" does (`browser_commands.cc`). Selected text has "Copy Clean Link to
    Highlight" after "Copy link to highlight" (`link_to_text_menu_observer.cc`, a new
    patch), as in Firefox: the link Chromium makes to the text, with the page's address
    cleaned (`Clean()` keeps the fragment, where the text's selector is), enabled once the
    link is made if there's something to clean.
  - The smoke test checks what "Copy link" copies from a page with tracking parameters, a
    link in one and a parameter only in Mozilla's list, which it downloads from the test's
    server, reading the clipboard from the page (headless, it's Oblada's own, not the
    system's).
