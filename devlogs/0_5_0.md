# Oblada 0.5.0: devlog

Released on 2026-10-27. Based on Chromium 156.0.8078.48. What changed since 0.4.2, in detail; the
public release notes are in [`changelogs/0_5_0.json`](../changelogs/0_5_0.json).

## The Barrier

Brave's protections of privacy, its Shields and privacy settings, in Oblada, named the
Barrier ("Barriera"). `oblada/browser/barrier/barrier.h` describes it; `PATCHES.md`'s
Barrier section lists its hooks in Chromium.

### Settings

- `barrier.{h,cc}`: what a site gets (`Settings`: up or down, ad blocking off, standard or
  aggressive, HTTPS upgrades off, standard or strict, scripts, fingerprinting and its
  exceptions by API, third-party cookies, forgotten when closed), from the profile's
  defaults (`oblada.barrier.ads`, `.https`, `.fingerprinting`, `.forget`; scripts and
  cookies are Chromium's own settings, the JavaScript content setting and
  `profile.cookie_controls_mode`) and the site's settings (`oblada.barrier.sites`, by
  eTLD+1). Incognito profiles read their profile's, and keep their changes in memory.
- Sites' scripts and third-party cookies are enforced by Chromium: `SetSiteOverrides()`
  writes the matching JavaScript and cookie exceptions (`SyncContentSettings()`), and
  `SwitchWatcher` rewrites them as the switch changes, in every profile.
- Third-party cookies are blocked by default: `RegisterChromiumDefaults()` sets
  `profile.cookie_controls_mode`'s default to `kBlockThirdParty` (from `browser_prefs.cc`,
  once Chromium's prefs are registered), so users who never chose keep Brave's default.
- The switch, `oblada.barrier.enabled` (Local State): `IsOn()` now, `WasOnAtStartup()` for
  what's decided at startup (`AddFeatureOverrides()`).
- Privacy settings that aren't by site, profile prefs: `gpc`, `strip_params`, `debounce`,
  `de_amp`, `reduce_language`, `sensitive_sites`, `show_count`; the social media embeds
  (`facebook_embeds`, `x_embeds`, `linkedin_embeds`) are in Local State, as filter lists
  are.

### Blocking trackers and ads

- Brave's engine, adblock-rust 0.13.3 (`third_party/rust/adblock`), added to Chromium's
  crates with gnrt (see `PATCHES.md`); `chromium/scripts/crates.sh` downloads them into the
  checkout, as `sparkle.sh` does Sparkle. `oblada/components/barrier/adblock`: the cxx
  bridge (`lib.rs`), Chromium's public suffix list for it (`domain_resolver.{h,cc}`), and
  `adblock::Engine` for C++ (`adblock_engine.h`).
- `engine_host.{h,cc}`: two engines, as Brave's `AdBlockEngineWrapper`: the default one
  with the lists "with first-party protections", whose matches don't count for a page's own
  requests out of aggressive blocking, and the additional one; on a sequence of their own.
- `filter_lists.{h,cc}`: Brave's catalog of lists (`lists/list_catalog.json`, kept up to
  date from brave/adblock-resources), each list downloaded from its own sources, again as
  it says (`! Expires:`) or daily; the browser's language's regional list turned on once;
  subscriptions by address; the user's own filters. The engines are compiled in the
  background and kept on disk ("Oblada Barrier" in the user data directory) with a key of
  their inputs, so a start without changes deserializes them. `--oblada-barrier-catalog`
  takes the catalog from elsewhere, for tests.
- The engines' resources, uBlock Origin's scriptlets and redirect resources with Brave's:
  `chromium/scripts/barrier_resources.sh` fetches uBlock Origin 1.75.0 and a commit of
  brave/adblock-resources (pinned by SHA-256) and `barrier_resources.mjs` puts them
  together as Brave's packager does, into the checkout (`third_party/oblada_barrier`, with
  its `README.chromium` for oblada://credits); `sources.gni` embeds them.
- `request_filter.{h,cc}`: a URLLoaderFactory in front of the network
  (`WillCreateURLLoaderFactory`), for frames, workers and navigations, on the engines'
  sequence. Blocked requests fail with `ERR_BLOCKED_BY_CLIENT` and count for their tab;
  `$redirect` ones get the resource (`net::DataURL`); `$removeparam` and tracking
  parameters go through a redirect (`RewriteLoader`); documents get `$csp` rules' policy
  (`CspClient`). Frames' WebSocket connections are checked as they open
  (`WillFilterWebSocket()`, `FilterWebSocket()`), then go on to extensions' WebRequest, or
  to the network. A page's own navigation is decided on the UI thread, which knows its
  site's settings (`DecidePage()`).
- CNAME uncloaking, as Brave: a request the lists let through is checked again under the
  names its host is an alias of (`CanonicalNameClient`), from a resolver of the storage
  partition's network context (the container's, `CreateHostResolver()`), with the request's
  network anonymization key; the request waits for it. The system's resolver gives the
  canonical name (`include_canonical_name`); with Secure DNS on its own, Chromium's DNS
  client gives the whole chain instead (canonical names only come from the system's,
  which doesn't use DNS over HTTPS). Not for addresses and localhost, nor, out of
  aggressive blocking, for the page's own site's hosts (standard blocking leaves those
  alone anyway); not at all when requests go through a proxy, which DNS queries don't
  (`ProxySettings`: a single proxy, a fallback one, one for HTTPS, or a PAC script, as
  Brave). Blocked under a canonical name, a request gets no stand-in.
- `tab_helper.{h,cc}`: what each tab blocked, for the button and the panel; the total in
  Local State (`oblada.barrier.blocked_total`).
- `domain_block.{h,cc}`: a page the lists block outright, opened as a page: with
  aggressive blocking, the Barrier's warning instead (served by the request filter), whose
  "Open anyway" carries a token of the session's; otherwise it opens, and is forgotten once
  closed if it had no cookies before ("unlinkable bouncing", `storage.h`).

### Cosmetic filters and scriptlets

- `oblada/common/barrier/barrier.mojom`: `BarrierAgent` (to each frame, before it commits:
  `BarrierPageSettings`) and `BarrierHost` (from frames).
- `cosmetic_filters.{h,cc}`: a navigation throttle holds a document's response until the
  engines gave its cosmetic resources; the tab helper sends them, with fingerprinting
  protection's settings, as it's ready to commit; `BarrierHostImpl` answers frames.
- `oblada/renderer/barrier/barrier_agent.{h,cc}`: once scripts may run in a new document
  (`RunScriptsAtDocumentStart()`, as extensions' document_start scripts: in
  `DidCreateDocumentElement()`, Blink silently drops them), the scriptlets run in the page,
  wrapped as uBlock Origin wraps them (`scriptletGlobals`), the selectors are hidden with a
  user style sheet, and `resources/cosmetic_filters.js` runs in an isolated world of its own
  (`ISOLATED_WORLD_ID_OBLADA_BARRIER`), with `obladaBarrier`'s native functions: procedural
  filters and actions, and the generic rules the page's classes and ids call for, which,
  out of aggressive blocking, leave alone what looks like the page's own content (Brave's
  heuristic: nothing from another site in it, and text).
- The element picker (`resources/element_picker.js`): the panel's "Block an element"
  highlights what's under the pointer, makes a selector of the one clicked (wider or
  narrower, editable), and keeps `host##selector` among the user's own filters.
  The page's context menu has it too ("Block an element", `kBlockElementMenuId`, in
  `render_view_context_menu.cc`), where the Barrier is up.

### Fingerprinting protection

- `farbling.{h,cc}`: the seed of a document, HMAC-SHA256 of a key of the profile's made at
  each start, the page's site and the frame's container (its storage partition); the
  exceptions by API, the site's and Brave's list's (`lists/webcompat-exceptions.json`).
- `oblada/blink/farbling.{h,cc}`, compiled in Blink's core: what each API gets, as Brave's
  standard protection; `ObladaFarblingSettings` on each `ExecutionContext`, set by the agent
  as documents are created (documents without settings of their own, like about:blank's,
  get their parent's) and passed on to dedicated workers and worklets. Shared and service
  workers ask the browser as they start (`WorkerContentSettingsProxy::GetObladaFarbling()`,
  then `ContentBrowserClient::GetObladaWorkerFarbling()`), which gives them their storage
  key's top-level site's settings, in their container (`GetWorkerFarbling()`, with
  `FarblingFor()`, which documents' settings come from too). See `PATCHES.md` for the
  hooks.
- What it changes: canvas readouts, audio samples, WebGL's and WebGPU's GPU names, cores
  and memory, the screen's size and the window's position, installed fonts (with languages
  reduced), the order of cameras and microphones, a made-up voice more for speech synthesis,
  made-up USB serial numbers, no keyboard layout, a full battery. `navigator.plugins` needs
  nothing: Chromium lists the same PDF viewers everywhere, nor does
  `navigator.storage.estimate()`: Chromium reports the site's usage plus 10 GB as its quota,
  the same everywhere (`kStaticStorageQuota`). The site's panel turns each off for the site.
- `oblada/blink/connection_pools.{h,cc}`: a site may have 30 WebSockets and 250 EventSource
  connections open at once, as Brave (`WebSocketChannelImpl::Connect()` fails the 31st as
  Chromium fails those over its own limit; `EventSource` aborts the 251st), against sites
  taking every connection to tell when others open one. Counted by page's site (a worker's
  own) in each renderer process; part of fingerprinting protection
  (`kConnectionPools`, which Brave's list of exceptions names `web-sockets-pool` and
  `event-source-pool`).

### Addresses

- `url_cleaning.{h,cc}`: Brave's query filter (`lists/query-filter.json`, and the
  parameters it keeps on some pages) on requests from another site or typed, and its
  debouncing (`lists/debounce.json`: redirect, base64, regex-path and template rules), with
  AMP caches' rules only when AMP pages are left for the original.
- `de_amp.{h,cc}`: AMP pages on publishers' own sites, as Brave: a navigation throttle reads
  the first part of each page's HTML the network gives (`NavigationHandle::GetResponseBody()`,
  which peeks at it), and, for an `<html amp>` (or `⚡`) page with a canonical link to
  another address, cancels it and loads the original in the same tab, with its referrer
  and transition. `DeAmpTrail` keeps the tab from going round in circles (7 hops at most,
  never back where it came from).
- `data_lists.{h,cc}`: Brave's data lists (the query filter, debouncing, sensitive sites,
  webcompat exceptions, HTTPS exceptions) are no longer only built in: they're downloaded
  daily from brave/adblock-lists (validated, then kept in "Oblada Barrier/data"), and their
  users parse them again when they change (`data_lists::Parsed`, thread-safe).
  `--oblada-barrier-data=<URL>` downloads them from elsewhere at once, for tests; with
  `--oblada-barrier-catalog` alone, they aren't downloaded.
- `sensitive_sites.{h,cc}`: Brave's list of sensitive sites (`lists/request-otr.json`: help
  against domestic violence and the like) open in a temporary container, which keeps
  nothing once closed, where Brave offers its off-the-record tabs. As
  `oblada.barrier.sensitive_sites` says: the Barrier asks first, with a page of its own
  instead of the site (served by the request filter; its answers carry the session's token),
  always reopens them there, or never. Tabs already in a temporary container, and incognito
  ones, are left alone.
- `google_sign_in.{h,cc}`: Google sign-in on other sites, as Brave's permission. The
  sign-ins that need Google's cookies in the site's pages (OAuth with a `storagerelay`
  redirect, Firebase's sign-in by redirect, which Brave tells apart the same way), opened by
  another site in a window or its own tab, get the Barrier's question first (a page of its
  own, served by the request filter, whose answers carry the session's token and the site),
  or, when `oblada.barrier.google_sign_in` is off or the site was refused, a page saying
  it's blocked. Allowed, the site's settings remember it (`google_sign_in`), and Google's
  sign-in host gets its cookies in the site's pages (a `COOKIES` exception for it under the
  site), which `SyncContentSettings()` keeps in step with the site's settings and the
  switch.

### Storage

- `storage.{h,cc}`: 30 seconds after a site's last tab in a container closes or goes
  elsewhere, what third parties stored under it (their partitioned cookies, and storage
  whose key has it as top-level site) when it blocks third-party cookies, and everything
  the site stored when it's set to be forgotten. `--oblada-barrier-forget-delay` shortens
  the wait, for tests.

### Network

- Global Privacy Control as the setting says, which content used to overwrite.
- Upgrades to HTTPS by site: off, Chromium's silent upgrades, or HTTPS-First's warning;
  Brave's list of sites that serve HTTPS badly isn't upgraded.
- Referrers capped at strict-origin-when-cross-origin, cookies at 6 months
  (`kObladaReferrerCap`, `kObladaCookieExpiryCap`), connections partitioned by site, HSTS
  upgrading pages themselves only (`kHstsTopLevelNavigationsOnly`, as Safari: other sites'
  requests can't read it back as a cookie), and
  Brave's list of web APIs turned off, unless the Barrier was off at startup.
- With languages reduced, `navigator.languages` has the first one and requests'
  Accept-Language it and its base.

### Settings > Oblada

- `settings/oblada_page.cc`: the Barrier's and privacy settings (among them WebRTC's IP
  handling, Chromium's `webrtc.ip_handling_policy`, which had no setting, and Do Not Track), the DANGEROUS switch (with
  a confirmation, and Relaunch when what's decided at startup differs), and two subpages
  (Settings' routes `/oblada/filters` and `/oblada/sites`, which `settings_main.html` passes
  as `route-path`).
- `settings/barrier_handler.{h,cc}`: the switch, scripts' default, the lists, subscriptions,
  the user's filters and the sites (with Google sign-in allowed or refused).
- Privacy: "Ask before signing in with Google on other sites" (`oblada.barrier.google_sign_in`).
- Settings' own `cr-*` elements come from its bundle: importing them again defines them
  twice, which stops the whole page.

### Tests

- `smoke.mjs`'s `barrierChecks()` (`OBLADA_SMOKE_ONLY=barrier` runs only them): test lists
  from the test's server; blocking, stand-ins, `$removeparam`, WebSockets, cosmetic filters
  (specific, generic, procedural, the site's own content left), scriptlets, GPC, stripped
  parameters, a debounced redirect (mapped to the test's server with
  `--host-resolver-rules`), fingerprinting (a canvas the same on a site and different on
  another and for real, cores, screen, languages, the made-up voice), a site with the
  Barrier down, third parties' storage and a forgotten site, Settings, and the switch.
- New checks: Brave's lists downloaded again (a parameter of the test's own stripped), a
  site's own AMP page left for the original (and a page with only a canonical link not),
  fingerprinting protection in dedicated, shared and service workers (the same as the
  page's, and not the real one), the limits to WebSockets and EventSource connections,
  Google sign-in (asked, allowed with its cookies in that site's pages only, refused then
  blocked, over HTTPS with a certificate of the test's own, as accounts.google.com), and,
  with the network, CNAME uncloaking (www.microsoft.com, an alias of Akamai's hosts).
- The restore check now names the restored tabs that loaded.
- Checked by hand, headless (sensitive sites need HTTPS, which the smoke test doesn't
  serve): the warning before a tracker's page and its way on, the question before a
  sensitive site and both its answers, the question before Google sign-in on another site,
  and the panel, the site panel and Settings' pages in English and Italian.

## Restored tabs that load at startup

`background_tab_loading_policy.cc` (`ShouldLoad()`): besides the tabs showing and pinned
ones, only tabs of sites known to work in the background (notifications allowed, or a title
or icon seen changing while hidden) load at startup. Chromium's
`UsesBackgroundCommunication()` also counts sites its site data knows nothing about, so the
first few restored tabs (`kMinTabsToLoad`, 4) always loaded: the smoke test's restore check
failed on 0.4.2's build too.
